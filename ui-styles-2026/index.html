Liquid Glass

Apple's translucent material for controls and navigation that reflects and refracts what sits behind it, approximated here in CSS with blur, tint and light.

Best for: the navigation and controls layer, floating above bold content.

Avoid when: the surface holds long text, or you would stack glass on glass.

Harbor Lights, mix 04

Sample playback card. Move your pointer over the glass and the specular sheen follows it. Turn on refraction to bend the lights behind the edges.

Refraction (experimental, Chromium only) Reduce transparency

  • Specular rim
  • Pointer highlight
  • Adaptive tint

.glass {
  position: relative;
  border-radius: 2rem;
  background: rgb(12 16 28 / 0.45);
  -webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(18px) saturate(1.6) brightness(0.9);
  backdrop-filter: blur(18px) saturate(1.6) brightness(0.9);
  box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 rgb(255 255 255 / 0.5), 0 24px 60px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.4);
}
.glass::after { /* specular rim */
  content: ''; position: absolute; inset: 0; padding: 1px; border-radius: inherit;
  background: conic-gradient(from 200deg, rgb(255 255 255 / 0.95), rgb(255 255 255 / 0.05) 22%,
    rgb(255 255 255 / 0.4) 50%, rgb(255 255 255 / 0.05) 74%, rgb(255 255 255 / 0.95));
  mask: linear-gradient(#000 0 0) content-box exclude, linear-gradient(#000 0 0);
  -webkit-mask: linear-gradient(#000 0 0) content-box xor, linear-gradient(#000 0 0);
}

Accessibility note

Main risk: text and control contrast over busy or moving backgrounds, and unclear tappable areas. Mitigation: this demo dims the photo and tints the glass so white text stays above 4.5:1 even over the brightest light, and the Reduce transparency switch or prefers-contrast: more makes every surface solid. This is a CSS approximation: true refraction is not possible in plain CSS, Apple publishes no official web CSS for it, and the optional lens is an SVG displacement filter that works mainly in Chromium.

Glassmorphism

Translucent frosted panels with background blur and a thin light border that create depth over vivid gradients.

Best for: sparing accents such as badges, cards and navigation over a background you control.

Avoid when: the panel carries long text over an intricate background.

Accessibility note

Main risk: text contrast depends on whatever is behind the panel. Mitigation: the panels use a strong white fill with dark text so the contrast holds over every blob, and they turn fully solid when backdrop-filter is unsupported or prefers-contrast: more is set.

Minimalism

Presenting content and features simply and directly, with flat surfaces, a limited palette and plenty of negative space.

Best for: content-first products and tasks that need focus.

Avoid when: stripping elements would hide controls or lower findability.

Accessibility note

Main risk: removing cues removes affordances and findability, which often shows up as pale grey text and faint controls. Mitigation: every control here keeps a dark visible border and text stays at 4.5:1 or better, with a clear accent focus ring.

Neumorphism

Elements share the page's background colour and use a light and a dark shadow to look softly extruded from the surface.

Best for: single-purpose panels such as smart-home controls and audio players.

Avoid when: the screen has dense forms, many actions or low-vision users.

Neubrutalism

Raw brutalist looks with modern type: thick dark borders, hard offset shadows with no blur and loud flat colour.

Best for: entertainment, consumer and creative brands.

Avoid when: speed and clarity are critical, as in healthcare.

Brutalism / raw web

Web design that deliberately rejects polish in favour of raw, unadorned layouts that make no effort to look comfortable or easy.

Best for: Brands that want to be memorable, especially in design, art, fashion and culture.

Avoid when: Finance, healthcare or banking, where clear navigation and structure matter most.

Bento Grid

A modular layout of distinct, rounded, differently sized content blocks packed into a compartmentalised grid, like the sections of a bento lunchbox.

Best for: Bite-sized content with a simple hierarchy, such as feature showcases, dashboards and portfolios.

Avoid when: There is no clear hierarchy, every cell is the same size, or there are more than about nine boxes.

Weekly summary

Three launches shipped and the backlog shrank by a fifth. Sample figures.

  • grid-template-areas
  • mixed spans
  • rounded corners

  display: grid;
  gap: 1rem;
  grid-template-columns: repeat(4, minmax(0, 1fr));
  grid-template-areas:
    "head head best  avoid"
    "head head card  card"
    "ctrl tags card  card"

}
.bento > * { border-radius: 28px; padding: 1.5rem; }
@media (max-width: 40rem) {
  .bento { grid-template-columns: 1fr; grid-template-areas: none; }
}

Accessibility note

Main risk: Differently sized tiles can make the visual order drift from the source order, which confuses screen reader and keyboard users. Mitigation: The HTML is written in reading order, the areas follow it row by row, and the grid collapses to one column on small screens.

Claymorphism

Soft, inflated, clay-like 3D interfaces built from rounded shapes, inner and outer shadows and pastel colours.

Best for: Friendly consumer, fintech, health and education products.

Avoid when: The interface is data-dense or needs a serious, enterprise tone.

Skeuomorphism

Interfaces that imitate real-world objects and materials, with textures, highlights and depth so that controls look tangible.

Best for: Hero objects, audio and tactile controls, games and onboarding where affordance matters.

Avoid when: The interface is a dense productivity tool or page weight is tightly constrained.

Spatial UI

A web approximation of visionOS-style depth, with translucent glass windows floating in layered space, gaze-like hover feedback and detached ornaments; "spatial UI on the web" is not a formally defined category.

Best for: Product showcases and media-style dashboards over a rich, controlled background.

Avoid when: Text sits on busy or variable backgrounds, the UI is critical, or devices are low-power.

Chrome / Liquid Metal

Mirror-like metallic surfaces with sharp reflections and high-contrast specular lighting, usually on dark backgrounds and used as an accent rather than a full-page treatment.

Best for: Automotive, fashion, music and luxury-tech campaigns that can feature one oversized metallic object or wordmark.

Avoid when: Copy is dense or functional, such as explainers, prices, deadlines, legal text and instructions.

Y2K

A glossy, optimistic look from the turn of the millennium, built from bubble shapes, translucent plastic, metallic and iridescent gradients and chrome stars, and now revived as a selective accent.

Best for: Fashion, beauty, music, social and youth brands, and launch teasers that commit to one hero cue.

Avoid when: Many effects would stack up, or reflective lettering would land on dense, financial, health or legal copy.

Retro / pixel

Interfaces that borrow 8-bit pixel art and 80s and 90s computing looks, such as bitmap fonts, flat limited palettes and hard-edged borders, inside a modern layout.

Best for: Leisure, creative and relaxation products for audiences that enjoy nostalgia and a fully committed look.

Avoid when: The product is enterprise software, the audience expects a sober tone, or the style would get in the way of the core task.

Editorial / Type-First

Large, expressive type is the main visual, set in magazine-style layouts of columns, rules and captions, with images and controls in support.

Best for: Content that needs authored rhythm, a cultural viewpoint or strong hierarchy.

Avoid when: The screen is full of tiny supporting text, dense utility controls or data tables.

Avoid when: Text would sit directly on the gradient, such as paragraphs or small labels.

A technical-drafting look of thin lines, fine grids, dimension marks and monospace labels on a blue ground.

Best for: Hardware, developer tools and infrastructure, product explainers, posters and instructional content.

Avoid when: The interface is content-heavy and built around long reading.

Accessibility note

Main risk: Thin light lines and light-blue text on navy can drop below readable contrast, and tiny monospace annotations are hard to read. Mitigation: Text is white or pale cyan on the blue at well above 4.5:1, annotations are real text at 0.8125rem or larger, and the line art keeps at least 3:1 against the blue (the WCAG 1.4.11 non-text contrast level).

Biophilic / organic

Nature-centred interfaces that use organic shapes, earthy colour, natural texture and gentle motion to feel calmer and warmer.

Best for: Wellness, sustainability, and calm or focus products.

Avoid when: The screen is a heavy data or operations tool.

Accessibility note

Main risk: Muted earth and green palettes, and text over nature imagery, can lose contrast, and ambient motion can distract. Mitigation: Text is deep forest green on opaque stone surfaces above 4.5:1, no text sits over imagery, and the leaves sway only when motion is allowed and can be stilled with a switch.
